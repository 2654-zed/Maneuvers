**Claim type:** OBSERVATION / DEDUCTION / INFERENCE / HYPOTHESIS / PREDICTION / SPECULATION, as the corpus *used* the claim, not as it labelled it. "OBSERVATION (reported)" means an incident fact adopted from third-party reporting.

**Confidence:** H/M/L is confidence in *the status assigned here*, with the basis stated in Notes; it is not a re-score of the claim.
**Later evidence:** drawn from `reports/evidence/` (on-chain checks, two verification reports, post-revision survey). Where later evidence does not actually test the claim, the ledger says so.

## Reading notes

1. The strongest claims in the corpus are its own on-chain reads (Kelp transaction, DVN configuration replay, Tornado funding) — all reproduced exactly.
2. The weakest are narrative claims about mechanism and attribution adopted from early press (THORChain), quotations and citations that cannot be located (Aethir dev.to sentences, Blockaid January prediction, Immunefi April 18), and validation claims built on category drift (V2 "6-of-14", V3 "predicted the surface").
3. A recurring structural fault: an inference is made from topology alone (high fanout ⇒ adversary; self-settlement ⇒ theft; dormant mass deployment ⇒ staging; prefix collision ⇒ control) and no benign base rate is measured. Correction #20 fixed one instance of this and the corpus did not propagate the lesson to the others.
4. The corpus's own corrections log is its best epistemic asset; its failure was propagation (retired figures live on in the lexicon) and scope (numbers were audited, narratives were not).
